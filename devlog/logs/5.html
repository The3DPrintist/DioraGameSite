The Last 90%

2/5/2026

"The first 90 percent of the code accounts for the first 90 percent of the development time.

The remaining 10 percent of the code accounts for the other 90 percent of the development time."

- Tom Cargill, Bell Labs


Its been 2 years and 4 days since the last Diora devlog. There's been a lot of burnout and crunch time since then. Luckily, theres no more mystery if the game is coming out, since its been out for over a month already! Yayyyyyyy. The first thing I'd like to say about Diora is that I'm really proud of how the game has been received, I've already gotten tons of positive feedback from the community.

I've had many people pop into the Diora chats to give feedback, its always great to see.


I have spoken a lot about burnout already, but I never felt that I didn't want to work on Diora, it was just hard to get back into it. My last year of college took a toll on my ability to focus on Diora. There was always something I should've been doing instead, usually homework or studying. I also felt pressured when playing games or working on other projects in my free time. After graduating and starting my full time job, I was finally able to get back into Diora, albeit slowly. With a full time job, I could finally ignore that stress when I was off the clock.

Around that time I learned about CiGDA, a local game dev group around Denver. I started going to their weekly meetups at a local coffee shop to work on Diora. This environment was really helpful for me, I was able to discuss our projects and get a few hours of productivity, (and banana oat bread). A few weeks later I finally felt that no matter what, I could finally say with confidence that Diora was going to happen. I sent an email to Panic giving them a short update, and some days later I had a deadline for a Demo, a promotional Video, and Release!


Once I was back into development, I was finally ready to take care of some of the biggest procrastinated features, Story and Level design. At this point I was worried that Diora wasn't fun, because it really didn't have much in the way of a playable game. I had exhausted working almost every other part of the game, so I finally started on levels. The first pass of levels was done very quickly, just throwing the elements down and making whatever came to mind. I knew these levels were going to be terrible, but It was still good to get something in the game, like a rough draft of an essay.

While this method made it easier to get a first pass of levels, I regret doing a playtest with them. Almost every level's feedback was 'meh', which was not helpful for me, and more importantly, was a waste of time for the people who were nice enough to help. I was determined to make sure the next round of playtesting was more representative of a finished game, so I was back to the drawing board.

This is the sheet we used to keep track of levels, which was very useful to understand what the game offered and kept us from forgetting about levels or overriding other peoples work. The photo is post development, so you cen see some levels that were dropped due to quality or time issues.


The demo was easy enough, but it was surprisingly helpful because it forced me to commit to the story that takes place at the beginning of the game. I polished up the intro and finally wrote the overarching story of Diora with some help from a friend. This made it much easier to work on story cut-scene sections later in the game as well.

The video shouldn't have been as much of a big deal either, but I am not great on camera and don't talk a ton, so I lost my voice from doing 60+ takes. I am still not happy with the video, (I'm so stiff), but at least the response was positive from the community! Diora Video from Playdate Update #8


After sending the demo and video out I flew to Portland for the Portland Retro Gaming Expo, where Diora was included at Panic's booth! The composer for Diora, (also Zac Snyder) flew out with me as well. Having the same name was funny to explain to people. We were able to get a tour of Panic's new office, which is so incredible it makes me want to take out a very large irresponsible loan for office space in Denver and start a game dev company. Panic's (new) Office Article

PRGE was fun even without including Diora being playable there, I enjoyed it much more than events like Denver's FanExpo. PRGE focuses much more on hardware and tech which was really exciting. I really enjoyed playing games and seeing the retro hardware running up close. I'll definitely be looking into returning to PRGE in the future.

It was really exciting to see Diora at an event of that size. I enjoyed talking the ears off the Panic employees as well.


After the PRGE weekend, I used the influx of hype from the trip to take a week off of work and get some much-needed crunch time development done. I took 12-ish hour days that week making levels, fixing bugs, making story sections, and making the end sequence. I was planning to read more articles and books about making good puzzles, but I never got to it before crunch time. I was stressed as we got closer to release, so I called on two of my developer friends to help with some levels. They were able to introduce some mechanics and puzzles I hadn't intended, which was great since it added much more depth, difficulty, and variety of levels in the campaign. We also would share levels with each other to give feedback and discuss what could be better. I think that the repetitive feedback helped make the levels playable and saved us from many of our somewhat uninformed puzzle/level designs.

After that week I was finally ready to do the second round of playtesting. Luckily it went well! There were still many levels with issues, but the feedback was much more positive. It was clear that we were on the right track, with feedback being positive with specific negatives, instead of negative with specific positives. We used this feedback to fix the biggest offenders, mainly levels that were difficult or confusing. We also completely remade some levels that were the worst offenders. After fixes and level changes, we asked the playtesters to retry the levels to see if it helped, and went back and forth with release candidate versions until we ran out of time. One of the biggest last minute changes was made to the boss fight, where I added mid-fight checkpoints, which the playtesters thought was needed. This was an important change but was stressful adding a feature like that just days from release. The levels aren't perfect and could've used some more work, but I am surprised about the amount of reviews praising the difficulty curve and puzzle design.

With a few days to spare, I sent out 1.0.0 to Panic and waited for the release, I was not very productive at work during that time. Luckily, there were no day-1 hotfixes, but I did need to remove a dev menu on the map shortly after release, ugh


Diora was already an exercise in extreme scope creep, and I think that a lot of that creep did service making Diora better, but I did need to drop some original concepts. I'd say the biggest dropped feature was a level sharing service. I still almost want to implement it, but it is a large undertaking and I don't think its incredibly necessary.

I also wanted to make a section of the game take place in a virtual world, with QR code signs and whatnot. This was scrapped early on, which was probably the right call.


Sorry this devlog was all over the place, it was hard to sum-up 2 years of work! I am planning to make more devlogs as well. People wanted a level design log, but this one already discusses it somewhat so I hope that's ok. I would love to make a devlog about the Art of Diora, not unlike the Logos from Planet Blip book, (a physical book like that would be cool but that sounds hard). If you'd be interested in a specific devlog, or any questions, feel free to ask me on the socials!

Thank you all for reading about and playing Diora, I love this community.
