The Last 90%

1/31/2026

"The first 90 percent of the code accounts for the first 90 percent of the development time.

The remaining 10 percent of the code accounts for the other 90 percent of the development time."

- Tom Cargill, Bell Labs


Its been 2 years and a day since the last Diora devlog. The first year of that gap was burnout, the second was crunch time. Luckily, theres no more mystery if the game is coming out since its been out for over a month already! The first thing I'd like to say about Diora is that I'm really proud of how the game has been received, I've already gotten tons of positive feedback from the community.

I've had many people pop into the Diora chats to give feedback, its always great to see.


I have spoken a lot about burnout already in a previous devlog, but I want to be clear I never felt that I didn't want to work on Diora. Finishing college took a toll on my ability to focus on these projects. I think the impact of college on my work was due to the feeling of always having something to work on at home. There was always *Something* I should've been doing instead, wether it was homework or or studying. After graduating and starting my full time job, I was finally able to get back into Diora, albeit slowly. With a full time job, I could finally ignore that stress when I was at home.


Once I was back into development, I was finally ready to take care of some of the biggest procrastinated features, Story and Level design. At this point I was worried that Diora wasn't fun, because it really didn't have much in the way of a playable game. I had exhausted working almost every other part of the game, so I finally started on levels. The first pass of levels was done very quickly, just throwing the elements down and making whatever came to mind. I knew these levels were going to be terrible, but It was still good to get something in the game, like a rough draft of an essay.

While this method made it easier to get a first pass of levels, I regret doing a playtest with them. Almost every level's feedback was 'meh', which was not helpful for me, and more importantly, was a waste of time for the people who were nice enough to help. I was determined to make sure the next round of playtesting was more representative of a finished game, so I was back to the drawing board.


Around that time I learned about CiGDA, a local game dev group around Denver. I started going to their weekly meetups at a local coffee shop to work on Diora. This environment was really helpful for me, I was able to discuss the work with them and have a few hours in a different environment which helped me focus. A few weeks later I finally felt that no matter what, I could finally say with confidence that Diora was going to happen. I sent an email to Panic giving them a short update, and some days later I had a deadline for a Demo, a promotional Video, and Release!


The demo was easy enough, but it was surprisingly helpful because it forced me to commit to the story that takes place at the beginning of the game. I polished up the intro and finally wrote the overarching story of Diora with some help from a friend. This made it much easier to work on story cut-scene sections later in the game as well.

The video shouldn't have been as much of a big deal either, but I am not great on camera and don't talk that much, so I lost my voice from doing over 60 takes. I am still not happy with the video, but at least the response was positive from the community!


After sending those out I flew out to Portland for the Portland Retro Gaming Expo, where Diora was included at Panic's booth! The composer for diora, (Zac Snyder) flew out with me as well. Us having the same name was funny to explain to people. We were able to get a tour of Panic's new office, which is so incredible it makes me want to take out a very large irresponsible loan for office space in Denver and start a game dev company. Panic's (new) Office Article

It was really exciting to see Diora at an event of that size, got to share a few business cards as well. PRGE was fun by itself of course, I enjoyed it much more than events like Denver's FanExpo. It focuses much more on Hardware and development which was much more interesting in my opinion. I'll definitely be looking into returning to PRGE in the future.


After the PRGE weekend, I planned to use the influx of hype from the trip to take a week off of work and get some much-needed crunch time development done. This plan went well, and I took 12-ish hour days that week polishing levels, fixing bugs, making story sections, and making the end of Diora. I was stressed as we got closer to release so I called on my friends to help with some levels. They were able to introduce some mechanics and puzzles I hadn't intended, which was very interesting. After that week I was finally ready to do the second round of playtesting, with hopefully some positive reaction from the group. Luckily it went very well, there were still a lot of levels with issues, but the feedback was much more positive. It was clear that we were on the right track, with feedback being positive with specific negatives instead of negative with specific positives.
